- **The Identification of the Dynamic Discrete-Choice Model with Stockpiling**  
  *Xinyao Kong, Jean-Pierre Dubé, Vaness Alwan*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1287/mksc.2024.1280`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2024.1280)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** We study the identification of the discount factor for dynamic discrete-choice (DDC) models of demand with stockpiling of a storable good. We show that the DDC has built-in exclusion restrictions that set-identify the discount factor without requiring any auxiliary assumptions. We compare this approach to previous work that assumes auxiliary structure on the storage cost function, clarifying how such structure generates exclusion restrictions and the corresponding data moments used for identification. Our key results show that these auxiliary restrictions are not necessary for identification; but that they are testable and can be included to achieve point identification and improve statistical efficiency. Using a case study of laundry detergent from earlier published work, we find a mean discount factor estimate of $0.957$, rejecting myopic models of choice in favor of the dynamic model. In counterfactual simulations, we show that some of the promotional lift during a discount period is indeed due to forward-buying. However, we predict considerably higher incremental sales compared to a standard discrete-choice model, which predicts that promotional discounts mainly cause substitution between brands. These results contribute to the long-standing discussion about the effectiveness of price promotions.

- **The Effects of Search Interface on Consumer Preferences: Evidence from a Randomized Experiment**  
  *Jia Liu, Olivier Toubia*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1287/mksc.2025.0261`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2025.0261)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** While structured search interfaces—such as filters and sorting tools—help consumers reduce search frictions on digital platforms, they also shape how consumers encounter product information during search. This paper studies how search interface design influences revealed preferences and market outcomes. We develop a theoretical framework linking interface design to attribute preferences through salience reallocation and information-based preference updating. The framework generates two predictions that can be formally derived: restricting faceted filters reduces the concentration of attribute importance weights (i.e., preference dispersion) and increases the diversity of products chosen (i.e., choice diversity). We test these predictions using a randomized, incentive-aligned online experiment in which 1,200 participants searched for wireless headphones on a custom-built e-commerce platform. Restricting filters led consumers to explore more broadly and resulted in more evenly distributed attribute importance weights and more diverse, higher-priced product choices. Mediation analysis further suggests that increased exploration was associated with shifts in attribute preferences. Our findings demonstrate that search interface design can construct, rather than merely reveal, consumer preferences.

- **Bargaining and Contract Forms in Distribution Channels: Why Do Slotting Fees Arise and Linear Contracts Persist?**  
  *Øystein Foros, Hans Jarle Kind, Greg Shaffer*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1287/mnsc.2025.03426`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.03426)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Two empirical patterns emerge in business-to-business settings, particularly in retail distribution channels such as grocery markets. First, linear contracts are widely observed alongside nonlinear contracts with fixed payments, despite the claimed efficiency advantages of the latter. Second, when fixed payments arise, they often flow from suppliers to retailers (slotting fees) rather than the reverse. We develop and analyze a model of multilateral bargaining in which a supplier negotiates separately with competing retailers to explain these patterns. The key mechanism is a trade-off between two distortions: under linear contracts, wholesale prices reflect bargaining power and can be either above or below the channel-profit-maximizing level, whereas under two-part tariffs, wholesale prices are set to maximize bilateral surplus independently of bargaining power but are systematically too low from an overall channel perspective because of rent shifting across relationships. As a result, neither contractual form uniformly dominates; instead, relative performance depends on how bargaining shapes gains from trade across firms. When bargaining leads to slotting fees, two-part tariffs yield higher channel profits than linear contracts, whereas when it would imply payments from retailers to suppliers, linear contracts may perform better. These findings offer a unified explanation for the persistence of linear contracts alongside two-part tariffs, the direction of fixed payments, and the conditions under which each contractual form is likely to arise.

- **EXPRESS: Correcting omitted-variable bias without instruments: An ICA-based alternative to Gaussian copula methods**  
  *Rouven E. Haschka, Florian Dost*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1177/00222437261499336`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261499336)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** This paper proposes an instrument-free correction for omitted-variable bias and overcomes a key limitation of Gaussian copula control function (GCCF) approaches. Omitted-variable bias is the most common source of endogeneity in marketing, and many articles apply GCCF to address it. Yet GCCF require a nonlinear regressor-error relation, whereas textbooks assume linear, additive relationships between regressors and omitted variables. The authors prove that GCCF is biased in classical omitted-variable settings. Extant articles that address omitted-variables with GCCF also show language cues implicitly assuming linear relationships, which invalidate the use of GCCF. The proposed endogeneity correction is tailored to this prominent case, utilizing independent component analysis (ICA). It assumes a latent normally distributed component that additively influences both the endogenous regressor and the error. This can be interpreted as the sum of omitted variables, which, according to the Central Limit Theorem, add up to a normal distribution. Through ICA, this component is identified and extracted from the endogenous regressor, rendering the regression model free of endogeneity and enabling causal inference. The paper proves that the model is point-identified despite endogeneity due to omitted variables, that the proposed estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal, and that Jackknife resampling yields valid standard errors.

  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1177/00222437261498165`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261498165)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** To guide consumers’ decisions regarding when to replace products such as water filters, printer ink cartridges, and engine oil, companies often provide visualizations of the product’s lifespan. We document a lifestage bias in which representing a product’s lifespan with more (vs. fewer) visually distinct stages increases replacement intentions even when the product’s actual usage is the same.
