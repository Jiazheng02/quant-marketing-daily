- **The Digital Journey of Health Insurance Marketing: Narrative Ads and Product Page Design**  
  *Yin Bai, Rui Wang, Xingyao Ren et al.*  
  `2026-11` · `doi:10.1287/mksc.2025.0168`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2025.0168)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Selling health insurance through digital advertising is fundamentally different from selling most consumer products online: health insurance protects against abstract future risks, and regulatory constraints prohibit transactions from closing on third-party platforms, forcing consumers to transition from the ad platform to the insurer’s own product page. We examine how aligning narrative ad content with product page design sustains persuasion across this mandatory handoff. In a randomized field experiment with a leading Chinese insurer on Douyin, we study 335 ads that generated nearly 17 million impressions and 777,412 click-throughs. Users who clicked were randomly routed to either a fact-based product page or one incorporating a customer experience exemplar. Purchase conversion rises most when narrative ads are paired with the exemplar page, increasing from 1.51% to 2.05%, implying roughly 8,400 additional policies per month at campaign scale. Neither element is effective in isolation. A causal mediation analysis confirms that sustained engagement on the product page accounts for more than 80% of the total effect. To address algorithm-driven selection in ad delivery and self-selection into clicking, we employ a sequential endogeneity correction strategy and confirm robustness with causal forests. Aligning ad design with product page design significantly improves conversion in regulated digital markets. History: Catherine Tucker served as the senior editor.

- **Banking on a Corporate Rebrand**  
  *Matthew Jaremski, Kris James Mitchener, Kilian Rieder*  
  `2026-11` · `doi:10.1287/mnsc.2025.03635`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.03635)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Firms often rebrand to counter negative shocks, but can it work? Using a historical natural experiment, we analyze a large sample of companies from the same industry (banking) that shared very similar names, but overwhelmingly decided to change them in response to a common, negative news shock. U.S. entry into World War I in 1917 created a sudden anti-German backlash against anything that invoked an association with the now enemy. The shock itself was thus orthogonal to bank fundamentals and pre-existing trends, but pushed banks to reconsider their brands. After 1917, the few German-named banks that kept their tainted names saw significant declines in assets, deposits, and market share relative to other banks. However, German-named banks that rebranded mitigated much of the negative shock. Specifically, German-named banks that adopted a new non-ethnic brand name avoided between 75% and 85% of the anti-German effect, and those that adopted a new patriotic brand name were able to neutralize it completely. Overall, we find that “crisis rebranding” paid off in our historical setting regardless of the chosen brand, with patriotic rebranding proving the most effective at offsetting the exogenous shock.
