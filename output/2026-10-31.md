  **Abstract:** Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to measure human preferences and opinions, but their outputs can diverge from human responses. We introduce human mimicry calibration (HuMCal), a post hoc, model-agnostic method that uses aggregate human benchmark data to learn how to combine LLM outputs from different prompts, personas, and model checkpoints. In applications to consumer transaction data and public opinion surveys, HuMCal improves agreement with human benchmarks over standard aggregation rules, and the calibrated weights often remain useful across time, geography, industry, and question clusters.

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Tip request messages are marketing communications that can help digital platforms balance customer spending with worker effort and compensation. We report the first field experiments testing how three appeals based on tipping motivations—injunctive norms (“It’s customary to leave a tip for the seller’s service”), personalized reciprocity, and impersonal reciprocity—affect tipping and future demand in a global freelance marketplace. Overall, the norms message increased tipping the most, and did so without reducing returning demand, showing that tip request messages can motivate customer tipping to better incentivize and compensate gig workers.
