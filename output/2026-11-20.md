  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.00747)

  <details>

  **Abstract:** The 0-1 knapsack problem is a fundamental problem of discrete choice that permeates many diverse fields and applications, but relatively little is known about what instances human solvers find more difficult than others. This paper reports the results of the first large-scale randomized controlled field experiment investigating this question, conducted in partnership with a global mobile gaming platform. A total of 100,000 different knapsack instances were predesigned for this study and randomly assigned to each player attempt at the knapsack problem. For our base-case analysis, there were 17,950,838 distinct player accounts who made a total of 150,284,195 submitted solution attempts. Contrary to expectations, players performed better on problems that were rated as more difficult by two traditional measures of knapsack difficulty (the Pearson correlation between value and weight vectors and Sahni complexity), but performed worse on problem instances that featured large optimality gaps from a suite of heuristic solution algorithms. These suggest that optimality gaps can serve as promising measures of human-perceived knapsack difficulty and potentially could be used to measure human-perceived problem difficulty for other types of discrete choice problems. We found evidence that players improved their performance with repeated attempts at the knapsack task overall, but less-experienced players of the mobile game improved more quickly than more-experienced players. Through the construction of a new measure of human-perceived difficulty rating for each knapsack instance, we found evidence that players who were assigned to instances with a higher difficulty rating in the past tended to perform better on future attempts. This paper was accepted by Manel Baucells, behavioral economics and decision analysis. Funding: S. J. Goh’s research is supported by a Startup [Grant R-314-000-110-133] and a 2021 Humanities and Social Sciences Faculty Research Fellowship from the National University of Singapore. - **The Demand for Government Debt**  
  *Egemen Eren, Andreas Schrimpf, Fan Dora Xia*  
  `2026-11` · `Vol.72(11)` · `doi:10.1287/mnsc.2025.00332`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00332)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** We document that the sectoral composition and marginal buyers of government debt differ notably across jurisdictions and over time. We use instrumental variables derived from monetary policy surprises to estimate the demand elasticities of various sectors. In the United States, commercial banks and mutual funds exhibit the most price-elastic demand, whereas the foreign official sector has a price-inelastic demand. Based on these estimates and under certain assumptions, we find that a 1% increase in the Central Bank holdings of U.S. Treasuries results in an around 8- to 13-basis-point drop in long-term yields depending on the market composition. Elasticities of individual sectors do not differ in a statistically significant manner when the Central Bank share in the Treasury market increases or decreases. However, different market compositions during various quantitative easing (QE) and quantitative tightening (QT) programs have led to an asymmetric effect with the impact of QE on yields being greater than that of QT. Our results suggest that, overall, the demand for U.S. Treasuries is considerably more elastic than for equities, corporate bonds, and emerging market sovereign bonds found in the literature. We also repeat the analysis for other jurisdictions and compare estimates for different sectors. This paper was accepted by Lukas Schmid, finance. Supplemental Material: The online appendix and data files are available at https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00332 .
