  **Abstract:** This study examines how a first-time malignant cancer diagnosis, acting as an informational shock to perceived longevity, affects the demand for life annuities. Using a quasi-experimental design and exploiting Swedish administrative data, we show that receiving a cancer diagnosis close to retirement reduces annuitization rates by 5.5%. The diagnosis lowers the money’s worth ratio of a life annuity by 33%, representing a substantial financial loss. Combined with the modest behavioral response, this yields a low demand elasticity of 0.17 with respect to the perceived annuity value. Evidence from a complementary laboratory experiment indicates that this limited adjustment is driven by the influence of a default option that reduces responsiveness to private health information and may disadvantage individuals in poor health. This paper was accepted by Camelia Kuhnen, finance.   `2026-11` · `doi:10.1177/00222429261511608`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222429261511608)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** A salesperson’s past performance is widely assumed to predict their future performance, and thus often guides managerial decision-making. Contrary to that assumption, this study uncovers why and under what conditions past performance becomes a less reliable predictor of future performance. A grounded theory study in a solution-selling context reveals that recent wins generate after-sales workload that lowers the odds of converting the next lead, particularly when those wins are complex, as occurs when customer relationships are new or procurement is formalized. Analysis of seven years of CRM data covering 3,669 sales leads at a Fortune 500 firm confirms that the link between past and future wins weakens as recent wins grow in complexity. Behavioral experiments causally confirm this attenuation effect, showing recent wins reduce effort on the next lead when those wins are complex. Finally, a single-dataset meta-analysis offers convergent evidence for workload as the underlying mechanism, revealing the relationship also weakens when workload is decoupled from winning and rooted in organizational change instead. For managers, these findings imply that using past performance to guide forecasting, quota setting, and coaching risks suboptimal decisions. They also underscore the importance of resourcing salespeople to balance after-sales responsibilities with new business development.
