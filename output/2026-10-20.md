- **The Motivational Paradox: How Aspirational-Goal Communications Influence Sales Performance and Information Acquisition**  
  *Doug J. Chung, Dongkyu Chang*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1287/mksc.2024.1051`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2024.1051)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** This study examines how aspirational-goal communications influence sales agents’ performance and information-seeking behavior. In a field experiment across 100 stores in Sweden, agents in the treatment group received daily personalized text messages with aspirational sales goals tailored to their prior performance. Results show that these communications increase sales performance, particularly among low-performing agents, primarily through higher unit sales. At the same time, they reduce agents’ willingness to monitor within-day sales progress—reflecting the ostrich effect—which can adversely affect performance. Information acquisition, in the form of sales-progress monitoring, typically aids effort provision, but aspirational-goal communications suppress such behavior, complicating performance management. These findings highlight the dual effects of aspirational-goal communications: while they enhance motivation, they may also prompt information avoidance. The study contributes to research on goal setting and sales management by clarifying the trade-offs in designing effective motivational and communication strategies.

- **AI in Disguise—Quasi-Experimental Analysis of a Large-Scale Deployment of AI-Generated Ads**  
  *Yannick Exner, Jochen Hartmann, Ziqian Ding et al.*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1287/mksc.2025.0547`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2025.0547)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** As Taboola prepared to launch its GenAI Ad Maker, a tool that lets advertisers create ad images within the platform, it faced a consequential question about whether AI-generated ad images would perform well with real consumers, and under what conditions. Working with Taboola, we construct a quasi-experimental comparison of 4,633 ‘sibling ads,’ meaning AI-generated and human-made images launched by the same advertisers, in identical campaign settings, at the same time. These ads span more than 369 million impressions and 2.5 million clicks. We do not find a detectable average click-through-rate disadvantage for AI-generated images relative to their human-made siblings. However, this average masks an important boundary condition: ads with AI-generated images outperform human-made ads when the AI images do not ‘look like AI.’ We identify key visual features influencing consumers’ perceptions of whether an ad looks AI-generated. While AI generates sharper, clearer, and less blurry images with larger faces, consumers associate these features with human-made ads. On the other hand, in line with consumers’ expectations, aesthetics, lower warmth, and intense color saturation in ads signal AI generation. These findings support Taboola’s roll-out communication, advertiser guidance, and continued refinement of the GenAI Ad Maker.

  `2026-10` · `Vol.72(10)` · `doi:10.1287/mnsc.2024.08149`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.08149)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** We use a unique data set of individual housing transaction histories from Norway and a novel shift-share design to show that temporary changes in the transaction sequencing decisions of moving homeowners—whether to buy first and then sell or vice versa—cause temporary local increases in house prices and seller liquidity. Our findings are consistent with a simple theory of how transaction sequencing decisions impact house prices in a frictional housing market. We also provide the first causal estimate of the elasticity of house prices to market tightness, which is around 0.1. Overall, our findings highlight the importance of trading frictions and supply-demand imbalances for housing market dynamics. This paper was accepted by Bo Becker, finance. - **Does Agglomeration Enhance Property Value?**  
  *Crocker H. Liu, Chen Zheng, Bing Zhu*  
  `2026-10` · `Vol.72(10)` · `doi:10.1287/mnsc.2023.02773`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.02773)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Does agglomeration within a building and/or neighborhood enhance or weaken property value? The valuation impact is unclear ex ante, given that agglomeration tradesoff enhanced productivity at the expense of a more concentrated tenant base. We find that a value premium exists only when agglomeration at both the building and neighborhood levels consists of firms in the same industry. This premium represents capitalized knowledge spillover externalities. Additionally, we show the stock market rewards real estate investment trusts that transact specialized buildings. The valuation consequences arising from agglomeration in the underlying real estate market are thus consistent with the public real estate market. This paper was accepted by Tomasz Piskorski, finance. Funding: This work was supported by the Real Estate Research Institute. Supplemental Material: The online appendix is available at https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.02773 .
