- **EXPRESS: Correcting omitted-variable bias without instruments: An ICA-based alternative to Gaussian copula methods**  
  *Rouven E. Haschka, Florian Dost*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1177/00222437261499336`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261499336)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** This paper proposes an instrument-free correction for omitted-variable bias and overcomes a key limitation of Gaussian copula control function (GCCF) approaches. Omitted-variable bias is the most common source of endogeneity in marketing, and many articles apply GCCF to address it. Yet GCCF require a nonlinear regressor-error relation, whereas textbooks assume linear, additive relationships between regressors and omitted variables. The authors prove that GCCF is biased in classical omitted-variable settings. Extant articles that address omitted-variables with GCCF also show language cues implicitly assuming linear relationships, which invalidate the use of GCCF. The proposed endogeneity correction is tailored to this prominent case, utilizing independent component analysis (ICA). It assumes a latent normally distributed component that additively influences both the endogenous regressor and the error. This can be interpreted as the sum of omitted variables, which, according to the Central Limit Theorem, add up to a normal distribution. Through ICA, this component is identified and extracted from the endogenous regressor, rendering the regression model free of endogeneity and enabling causal inference. The paper proves that the model is point-identified despite endogeneity due to omitted variables, that the proposed estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal, and that Jackknife resampling yields valid standard errors.

- **EXPRESS: Why are products so similar despite diverse consumer needs? The interplay between advertising and horizontal product differentiation**  
  *Amit Pazgal, David Soberman, Raphael Thomadsen*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1177/00222437261500395`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261500395)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Firms are often less differentiated than standard price-competition models predict. We explain this gap by showing how firms can use informative or reminder advertising to soften competition. When firms choose locations, advertising, and prices endogenously, they may choose to soften price competition through limited advertising rather than greater product differentiation, allowing them to locate closer together while limiting price competition. This outcome of low advertising and central collocation is especially likely when advertising costs are high. We also observe that partially-differentiated equilibria, with one firm locating centrally and the other at an edge, and full differentiation, can each emerge under certain conditions. Thus, firms endogenously choose a mix of advertising and product differentiation to moderate price competition. Further, the shape of travel costs influences the equilibrium outcome. If the disutility from distance is highly convex, such as occurs when consumer preferences exhibit satisficing, central collocation becomes the unique equilibrium when more consumers are concentrated at the edge locations. However, when the disutility from distance is linear, having more customers located towards the edge locations makes collocation less likely. Overall, the interaction of advertising costs, consumer preferences, and location choices help explain why firms often forgo product differentiation.
