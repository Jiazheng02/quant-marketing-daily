  **Abstract:** While structured search interfaces—such as filters and sorting tools—help consumers reduce search frictions on digital platforms, they also shape how consumers encounter product information during search. This paper studies how search interface design influences revealed preferences and market outcomes. We develop a theoretical framework linking interface design to attribute preferences through salience reallocation and information-based preference updating. The framework generates two predictions that can be formally derived: restricting faceted filters reduces the concentration of attribute importance weights (i.e., preference dispersion) and increases the diversity of products chosen (i.e., choice diversity). - **EXPRESS: Correcting omitted-variable bias without instruments: An ICA-based alternative to Gaussian copula methods**  
  *Rouven E. Haschka, Florian Dost*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1177/00222437261499336`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261499336)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** This paper proposes an instrument-free correction for omitted-variable bias and overcomes a key limitation of Gaussian copula control function (GCCF) approaches. Omitted-variable bias is the most common source of endogeneity in marketing, and many articles apply GCCF to address it. Yet GCCF require a nonlinear regressor-error relation, whereas textbooks assume linear, additive relationships between regressors and omitted variables. The authors prove that GCCF is biased in classical omitted-variable settings. Extant articles that address omitted-variables with GCCF also show language cues implicitly assuming linear relationships, which invalidate the use of GCCF. The proposed endogeneity correction is tailored to this prominent case, utilizing independent component analysis (ICA). It assumes a latent normally distributed component that additively influences both the endogenous regressor and the error. This can be interpreted as the sum of omitted variables, which, according to the Central Limit Theorem, add up to a normal distribution. Through ICA, this component is identified and extracted from the endogenous regressor, rendering the regression model free of endogeneity and enabling causal inference. The paper proves that the model is point-identified despite endogeneity due to omitted variables, that the proposed estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal, and that Jackknife resampling yields valid standard errors.

  </details>

  *Amit Pazgal, David Soberman, Raphael Thomadsen*  
  `2026-10` · `doi:10.1177/00222437261500395`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261500395)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Firms are often less differentiated than standard price-competition models predict. We explain this gap by showing how firms can use informative or reminder advertising to soften competition. When firms choose locations, advertising, and prices endogenously, they may choose to soften price competition through limited advertising rather than greater product differentiation, allowing them to locate closer together while limiting price competition. This outcome of low advertising and central collocation is especially likely when advertising costs are high. We also observe that partially-differentiated equilibria, with one firm locating centrally and the other at an edge, and full differentiation, can each emerge under certain conditions. Although the total price (e.g., $40) is always provided, the associated rate information is not (e.g., $8/lb of coffee). The focus of this research is on understanding the role that these rates play in influencing consumer decision-making. The authors find that regardless of whether rates are provided by managers or calculated by consumers, consumers are more sensitive to promotional offers with dollar per unit (vs. unit per dollar) rates, because dollar per unit rates increase price salience.
