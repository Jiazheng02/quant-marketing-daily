  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** While structured search interfaces—such as filters and sorting tools—help consumers reduce search frictions on digital platforms, they also shape how consumers encounter product information during search. This paper studies how search interface design influences revealed preferences and market outcomes. We develop a theoretical framework linking interface design to attribute preferences through salience reallocation and information-based preference updating. The framework generates two predictions that can be formally derived: restricting faceted filters reduces the concentration of attribute importance weights (i.e., preference dispersion) and increases the diversity of products chosen (i.e., choice diversity). We test these predictions using a randomized, incentive-aligned online experiment in which 1,200 participants searched for wireless headphones on a custom-built e-commerce platform. Restricting filters led consumers to explore more broadly and resulted in more evenly distributed attribute importance weights and more diverse, higher-priced product choices. Mediation analysis further suggests that increased exploration was associated with shifts in attribute preferences. Our findings demonstrate that search interface design can construct, rather than merely reveal, consumer preferences.

  **Abstract:** As Taboola prepared to launch its GenAI Ad Maker, a tool that lets advertisers create ad images within the platform, it faced a consequential question about whether AI-generated ad images would perform well with real consumers, and under what conditions.   **Abstract:** We quantify the short-term impact of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) on sales performance through a series of large-scale randomized field experiments involving millions of users and products at a leading crossborder online retail platform. Over 2023–2024, the platform integrated GenAI into seven business workflows spanning customer service, consumer-product matching, advertising, and seller services. We find that GenAI adoption increases sales in most workflows, with effects ranging from no detectable impact to 16.3% depending on GenAI’s marginal contribution relative to baseline firm practices.   *Matthew Jaremski, Kris James Mitchener, Kilian Rieder*  
  `2026-11` · `doi:10.1287/mnsc.2025.03635`

  **原文链接：** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.03635)

  <details>
  <summary><b>展开详情</b></summary>

  **Abstract:** Firms often rebrand to counter negative shocks, but can it work? Using a historical natural experiment, we analyze a large sample of companies from the same industry (banking) that shared very similar names, but overwhelmingly decided to change them in response to a common, negative news shock. U.S. entry into World War I in 1917 created a sudden anti-German backlash against anything that invoked an association with the now enemy. The shock itself was thus orthogonal to bank fundamentals and pre-existing trends, but pushed banks to reconsider their brands. A quasi-experiment reveals that, despite ATS’s flexibility and apparent alignment with firm objectives, an ATS-to-ITS transition improved performance , with larger gains among lower-performing and higher-span territories. A scenario-based experiment with sales managers suggests that these effects may stem from the reallocation of resources from high to low performers but provides no evidence of an increase in managers’ overall resource investment. A survey of sales leaders indicates that a sales star bias —a managerial tendency to overallocate resources toward high performers relative to their marginal returns—likely drives suboptimal resource allocation under the flexible ATS. Finally, a supplementary field study documents that, following an ATS-to-ITS transition, managers increased joint fieldwork , especially with the lowest performers, and performance again improved. These results provide insights into managerial incentive design and offer guidance for firms.
